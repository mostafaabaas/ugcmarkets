Guide · Mechanics

How UGC marketplaces work: from brief to approved delivery

A UGC marketplace is a structured way to buy content services. Instead of searching social platforms for freelancers and negotiating in private chats, the brand publishes one brief in a category, and providers who do that craft answer with offers — price, timing and scope, in the open.

Once an offer is accepted, the deal gets its own record: the funded amount, the conversation, the files and the approval. That record is what makes the process fair — both sides can point to the same brief, the same delivery and the same decision.

  • ✓The brief is the contract's plain-language twin — if it is not in the brief, it is not in the deal.
  • ✓Offers are comparable — same brief, same fields, so price is never the only difference.
  • ✓Approval ends the deal — delivery plus approval is what moves the money.
Guide · Payments

Escrow payments for creative work, explained

Escrow means a neutral middle step for money: the buyer pays the amount in, a platform holds it, and it is released to the seller when the agreed condition happens. In creative work on UGC Markets, that condition is the brand approving the delivery.

Creative work needs this more than most trades because the product is a file, not a box: without escrow, one side always carries all the risk — the provider works hoping to be paid, or the brand pays hoping to receive. Escrow removes the hoping from both sides.

  • ✓The provider starts against funded money — no unpaid spec work.
  • ✓The brand reviews before release — revisions are requested inside the deal, on the record.
  • ✓No approval, no release — the condition is simple and known to both sides from the start.
Guide · Briefs

How to write a creator brief that gets good offers

Providers price risk. A vague brief — "we need some videos for our product" — forces every provider to guess, and guesses come back as high prices, long timelines or silence. A specific brief gets precise, competitive offers because the work is finally measurable.

Specific does not mean long. It means the six facts a provider needs are present, in plain words, before they are asked for.

  • ✓The product and the audience — what it is, who it is for, in which market.
  • ✓The exact deliverable — format, length, count and where it will run.
  • ✓References and must-nots — two examples you like, and the claims that are off-limits.
  • ✓Deadline and usage rights — when it is due, and whether it will run as an ad, for how long.
Guide · Saudi market

Booking Saudi creators through a UGC marketplace: what Gulf brands check first

A brand selling in Saudi Arabia does not buy "a creator" — it buys a voice its audience recognises: Saudi dialect spoken naturally, the platforms Saudi audiences actually live on (short video and Snapchat alongside Instagram and TikTok), and content that respects local norms without sounding like a translated ad. A marketplace makes those checks practical because offers arrive in one place, against one brief, with profiles and past work attached.

Run the deal the marketplace way: the brief names the Saudi audience and dialect, the deliverables and the usage scope (organic posting, paid ads, duration, territory); offers come back with samples in the same dialect; the deal is funded into escrow before filming starts; drafts are reviewed in the deal chat; and payment is released on approval of the delivery — not on a promise. Brands that skip the dialect and usage checks pay for them later, in reshoots and takedowns.

  • ✓Dialect before followers — a Saudi voice your audience trusts beats a bigger name speaking past it.
  • ✓Name the platforms — state where the content runs, including Snapchat where your audience lives there.
  • ✓Usage in writing — ads, duration and territory settled in the brief, before filming.
  • ✓Funded before filming — escrow first, approval releases: no unfunded starts, no unpaid deliveries.
FAQ

What brands and providers ask first

The essentials, answered plainly — the marketplace, the money and the waitlist.

What is UGC Markets?

UGC Markets is a marketplace for content services: a brand posts one brief for the UGC or production work it needs, and service providers — creators, videographers, photographers, editors and studios — answer with offers. The whole deal then runs inside the marketplace, from brief to approved delivery.

Who can offer services on UGC Markets?

Independent creators and production professionals: UGC creators, videographers, photographers, video editors, designers and small studios. Providers join with a public profile of their services and rates; identity-verified providers carry a badge, and brands can review a profile before accepting any offer.

How does the escrow payment work?

When a brand accepts an offer, it funds the deal amount into the platform wallet. The money stays held while the provider works. The brand then reviews the delivery and either requests a revision or approves it — approval is what releases the payment to the provider. Nobody works unpaid, and nobody pays for work they have not received.

What does it cost to join?

Joining the waitlist and creating a profile are free, for brands and for providers. The terms of any deal are shown in writing before a brief is funded, so nothing is ever charged by surprise.

Which countries does UGC Markets serve?

The marketplace opens for Egypt, Saudi Arabia and the Gulf. Every brief states its market and category, and offers come from providers who actually work in that market.

When does UGC Markets open, and how do I join?

The marketplace opens in waves, starting with the waitlist. Join through the booking button or WhatsApp, tell us whether you are a brand or a provider, and we bring you in as your category opens.
